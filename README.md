# @assembly

Parts, placements, steps and guides as literals — the ERP half (parts, a kit, a bill of materials) and the LMS half (a guide, step by step) of building a concrete product from parts. Composition is part + placement; process is guide + step; they meet only by slug. The domain a product package such as `@droneaid` builds on; it carries no product of its own.

## the ontology

A literal is one of four; its one TOPOGRAPHICAL symbol says which, and the runtime stamps it as `ontology`. What the writer hardcodes about an ontology — the traits it may carry, whether it is a layer — is `ONTOLOGIES` in `domain/assembly/schematics.js`, read by `mint`, rendered by a harness, drawn as a form by the editor:

| ontology | traits | |
|---|---|---|
| `part` | `LABELED SOURCED MODELED PARAMETRIC COUNTED` | what a thing IS — geometry of its own (a GLB on the freight, metres, Y-up, or a generator), a kit count when you order it, an interior when placements name it as their layer |
| `placement` | `LABELED PLACED JOINED` | one occurrence of a part under a part's layer — which layer, its name among its siblings, what it places (never absent), where. A fastener's placement says what it joins; how far it is done up is a step's note |
| `step` | `LABELED ASSEMBLED INSTRUCTED ANNOTATED STAGED` | one unit of a guide: the placements it installs by slug (`ASSEMBLED`), its numbered lines and pictures (`INSTRUCTED`), its callouts (`ANNOTATED` — `note.tip`, `note.warning`, the `warn.*` family), how the stage shows it (`STAGED` — camera, explode, ghost, what counts as built, tints). Its `tool.*` symbols are what it lays out; what it lays out in parts is derived by the fold |
| `guide` | `LABELED SEQUENCED INSTRUCTED ANNOTATED STAGED` | the part it builds and its steps in sections (`SEQUENCED { subject, sections }`), an intro, callouts, the overview's staging. Step slugs are `<guide>.NN` |

**A slug is unique in the daemon** — one name, one literal; a naming conflict is answered by depth or a number, never by a second owner. A literal may carry the mode that minted it as an annotation; nothing resolves a literal by anything but its slug. **A placement's slug is its layer and its path** (`frame.arm.0`, `rack.arms.arm.1`), so the same layer, parent and name again is the same literal — an over, carrying only what changes. `active: false` removes it from that layer on. Two ways to nest, never blurred: `parent` is structure inside ONE layer; `ref` naming a part with an interior grafts that part's composition under the placement's path.

`resolve(rows, part)` folds a part's composition into a glTF-shaped tree; `resolve(rows, guide, { upto })` folds the guide's subject with each placement's `at` (the first step naming it) and `active` (the steps' last opinion through `upto`), and per step what it lays out — its `tool.*` symbols, and the parts the bill gains at that step. `bom` counts what the build uses against `COUNTED`, the kit, sealing a bought sub-assembly; for a guide, what is active. All pure (`domain/assembly/resolve.js`). A step is folded through its guide. A sub-assembly placed twice is one slug in a step: naming it installs both.

## what the daemon mounts

The barrel (`domain/assembly/assembly.viva.js`) is what the runtime reads of a domain and nothing else: `traits` · `schematics` · `aperture` · `tools` · `manifest` with `EXPOSED` + `TOOLING`. A mode never imports outside its directory and reaches a domain's shape at runtime as `daemon.domain.schematics.PLACED`. The folds and the generators stay files, imported by path.

- **aperture** (`aperture/index.js`) — one repository verb per nature, the subject a slug in the input: `/part /step /guide` mint or over (each list a step or a guide carries is given whole) · `/rename` gives a part, a step or a guide a new slug, everything naming it following — a step's placements, anchors, tints and built, a guide's subject and sections · `/duplicate` copies under the next free slug (a guide takes its steps, `<guide>.NN` → `<copy>.NN`; a step lands after the original) · `/placement /move` edit a part's layer · `/sequence` sets a guide's sections whole · `/resolve /bom /closure /tree` fold and list · `/retract` unmints (refused while something not named still references one; `closure: true` takes a root with its interior, never the inside of a part something else still places; `dry: true` answers what would go) · `/literals` cards · `/geometry` generates. The runtime slurps them into the daemon's root and builds `daemon.call` from them.
- **tools** (`tools/`) — ten of the same verbs armed as `assembly_*` for every mode's agent in the daemon: `part step guide rename duplicate placement move sequence resolve bom`. A tool's input is its nature's input, one description; each calls its verb through `daemon.call` with the caller and answers `{ message, entities: { literal }, object }`.

## the seams

- `traits/literal/` — literal traits, never an entity: `rows.js` the READS (`placedOf · joinsOf · subjectOf`, a guide's steps are `resolve.js` `order`); the verbs on the trait that owns them — `placed.js` the graph, `counted.js` `part · bom`, `assembled.js` `step`, `sequenced.js` `guide · sequence`, `claims.js` payload shapes only; a literal minted without a calling mode belongs to the topology that ships its ontology's root symbol. The repository is the ONLY writer (`part · step · guide · rename · duplicate · placement · move · sequence · retract`), maintains `uses` (a placement uses its ref AND its layer, a guide its subject and its steps, a step every slug it holds; `in` is the reverse), and folds (`closure · resolve · bom · tree`). A retract is refused while a step names a placement or a guide names a step; a guide's closure takes its steps, never the build. An over merges what it carries; `symbols` present is the family set again.
- `schematics.js` — `ONTOLOGIES`, the `v` shapes that type every payload (one per trait + `GENERATOR PATH`), `VERBS` (what each verb takes, shared by nature and tool), `TREE / ROW / SCENE` (what a screen lists).
- `generators/` — `fastener.bolt · standoff · nut`: params schema + `build(params) → Object3D`, metres, no DOM; served as three.js JSON by `/geometry`.
- `topologies/{part,placement,step,guide}` — each ships its ontology's root symbol and the universal vocabulary: `material.* source.*` · `joint.*` · `note.* warn.* tool.*`. A product's own families come with its topography; a guide's sections are its own content, not symbols.

## the importer — `@assembly/editor/import`

Model files into the domain, in **two stages**: a **dry parse** that reads everything the file yields in the file's own units and frame, then the operator's choices, then a **live parse** that decodes again under those choices and mints. Drop `glb · gltf · blend · usd · usdz · usdc · fbx · obj · stl · 3ds · step · iges` on the screen; each file is a **job** walking **sniff → validate → decode → review → commit**, every line of its decode log streamed as it happens.

- `/parse` — `POST` the file as the body, its name in `x-viva-name`. Yields `EVENT` lines; the DONE line carries `META` and the `READING`: every mesh object with its box and placement in **source units**, what the file **declares** (unit, up axis), what was skipped, materials, triangles. Nothing is minted; the file and its per-object GLBs sit on `intake/<stem>/`. `sniff.js` reads magic bytes before the extension; `decode.js` runs `cad.py` (FreeCAD) for a b-rep, then `harvest.py` (blender headless) for everything.
- **review** — no verb; the stage shows the dry parse's GLBs placed at their placements × the unit knob. `fold.js` is pure and runs in the screen. The knobs, each shown as *file declares · your choice · what results*: `into` (the root part's name — **every import is its own**, a slug already there is suffixed) · `unit` · `up` · `fold` · `groups` · `hidden` · `origin` · `chord`.
- `/commit { name, settings }` — every setting required. Decodes again under the settings, folds objects into parts by the chosen rule, and mints through the domain: `/part` per folded part (`SOURCED · MODELED parts/<slug>.glb · COUNTED`), a part for the root and one per collection, `/placement` per object into its collection's part. The DONE line carries what was minted.
- `/revert { slugs }` — a commit taken back through `/retract`, the GLBs off the mountpoint; the job drops to parsed.
- `/tree { slugs }` — the domain's `/tree`, scoped to what this buffer committed (`[]` reads nothing).

## the editor — `@assembly/editor/assembly`

Create, read, update, delete, place, move, retract — the composition: parts and placements. Guides and steps show in the tree as roots that open their subject; they are authored in `@assembly/editor/guide`. Four panes on one buffer, each toggled from the header, separated by dragged seams; every colour is a dapper token, so the editor follows the theme:

- **tree** — two scopes. `daemon` lists every model the daemon holds, a part nothing places, with an ontology filter and a search; a click on a model previews it on the stage. `buffer` lists the model in view, rooted at its outermost edge, with `root` · `up` and the crumbs of the subject's way up; a row's `subject` button narrows the stage to it without moving the root. Above both, the row that creates: `new` (a model in the daemon, a part placed into the subject in the buffer) · `dup` · `rename` · `delete`.
- **stage** — the subject folded `upto` a step, each placement a group named by its path, composed against its parent's frame. `translate · rotate · scale` on a `TransformControls` gizmo, **one `/placement` over per release**; `joint` picks the placements a fastener holds and writes `JOINED` with a torque. The pick reads as a translucent solid in a cage, everything else ghosted; `frame` fits the fold, `focus` fits one literal.
- **inspector** — the form, the symbol chips, save, retract, and for a placement the move panel.
- **harness** — three columns underneath: `context` (what the agent is given — the subject, the selection, the last written literal, the focus; each can be dropped), the composer (lines, one verb each, run in order through the same natures — one turn on screen at a time, walked with ◀ ▶), and `activity` (every call the screen made, and whether it is running, done or failed).

There is no dock in the buffer. The conversation is anima's own integrated dock beside it; the mode is `CONVERSATIONAL` + `HARNESSED` so that dock reaches this harness and these tools.

The inspector's form is **drawn from the domain's schematic**: `/schematics` serves `ONTOLOGIES · TRAITS · VERBS` as JSON schema, `buffer/form.js` turns the verb's schema into fields (kind from the schema, example from the description; a trait payload with its own properties becomes dotted fields like `sourced.object`), the card's traits into values, and the edited values into the verb's input — the identity always, then only what changed. `symbols` are chips from `/vocabulary`. Delete asks first: a placement goes with what sits under its path and the part it places stays; a part lists exactly what `/retract` with `dry` says would go; a step leaves its part's sequence.

**Driving it.** A click selects; the cursor is separate and walks with `j k ↑ ↓ g G`, `l/→` expands or descends, `h/←` collapses or ascends. In the buffer, **selection never changes the subject**; in the daemon, a model clicked is a model previewed. **Opening descends**: a placement opens *what it places*, so any layer of the hierarchy becomes the subject on its own. Ways in: `Enter`/`o` on the cursor row · a double-click · a row's `subject` button · a double-click on the mesh. The way out: `u`/`Backspace` (from the root, back to the daemon), `root`, or a crumb. `space`/`x` toggles a row into the selection, `f` focuses it, `n d r Delete` are new · duplicate · rename · delete, `Esc` peels dialog → notice → rename → selection → up, `/` searches the daemon, `:` the composer, `[ ]` walk `upto`, `1-3` set the filter. Tree, stage and context share one cursor and one selection.

The harness (`harness.js`) puts three sections in the system prompt on every call — `[Ontology]` rendered from the same schematics, `[Vocabulary]`, `[Screen]`: the open buffer's subject folded as lines (`path ← ref (slug) @ [x, y, z]`), the selection, the focus, the last written literal, or plainly that nothing is open, with the daemon's roots named so the model can say one. Three tools of its own — `open { slug, upto? }`, `select { slugs }`, `focus { slug }` — write the thread's newest buffer of this mode; the domain's nine change the literals. **The whole screen lives in `buffer.data`** — subject · upto · selected · scope · filter · the search · the expanded tree keys · the cursor · focus · desk · gizmo · the joint draft and its torque · the inspector's unsaved draft and its move fields · a rename typed and unrun · the composer's text, its turns and which one is shown · the activity · which panes are shown and how wide. `retain(patch)` sets `buffer.data` and persists it, debounced for typing and seam drags. A buffer you come back to is exactly where you left it; a fresh one is empty because it is created with `data: {}`.

## the guide — `@assembly/editor/guide` · `@assembly/player/guide`

Two modes over the same two literals, from one design. Both read one door, `/read { guide }` — the guide, its steps in order with what each lays out, the subject's fold with each placement's step, the parts' cards, the symbols' names — and draw the same **stage kit** (`buffer/kit/`, copied into each, pinned byte-equal by `tests/kit.test.js`): a GLB per part from the cargo, one group per placement composed through its parent frame, a status per placement (fresh · solid · ghost · hidden), a tint per placement, explode, settle, named views that swing round the model, a view cube, focus, a camera snapshot. Every hue is a theme token (`P-hex-zero`).

- **player** — the builder's walk: the subject full-bleed, a floating panel with the guide's sections and the step on screen expanded — the parts it lays out as chips, numbered lines that frame their anchors on the stage, notes, pictures with a lightbox, tools — Back · Next · Completed. `buffer.data`: `guide step done explode ghost view focus lightbox`. Harness: a mentor with `[Guide]` as written and `[Screen]`; one tool, `step { step }`.
- **editor** — the author's hand: the steps column in sections (rename, add, delete when empty, drag steps and sections), the stage with a selection card (pull into the step, remove, go to its step; ⌥-drag box-selects), an inspector with a Guide tab (subject picker, name, description, intro, notes, camera, inventory) and a Step tab (prerequisites — every earlier step, or custom with tints; the parts list — this step · kit · all; instructions; notes with a kind; camera; media from the cargo's `manual/*.webp`; tool chips). **Auto-save**: the draft lives in `buffer.data.draft` and lands in its literal 700 ms after the last keystroke — `/step` or `/guide` over, the lists whole; a step switch commits first. A fresh buffer lists the daemon's guides and `+ new guide`. Harness: `[Ontology]` (guide and step), `[Guide]`, `[Screen]`; tools `open { guide, step? }` · `select { slugs }`.
